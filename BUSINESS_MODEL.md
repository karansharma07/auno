# AUNO — Monetisation architecture (Dubai-first)

*Founder strategy / working hypotheses, not legal or financial advice. All proposed prices require actual user, partner and legal validation.*

## Value creation before monetisation

Core service: build a trusted network where people find excellent recurring activities and real friendships. Resident value = belonging + discovery + convenience. Visitor value = safe local discovery + time-aware journeys + optional utility services. Business value = attendance, booking operations, customer acquisition and retention. Trust and privacy are *core product costs*, not premium benefits.

## Transaction economics — worked hypotheses

Example A: AED 80 sports booking, 12% retained = AED 9.60 gross platform fee. If AUNO bears an *illustrative* 2.9% + AED 1 processing charge on the entire ticket, payments cost AED 3.32, leaving AED 6.28 before customer support, refunds, tax, fraud, host acquisition and other variable costs. This is **not** company profit.

Example B: AED 25 luggage booking, 20% retained = AED 5.00 gross platform commission. Same hypothetical card rate costs AED 1.73, leaving AED 3.27 before claims, insurance, support, refunds and technology. Luggage cannot carry the business economics alone on small baskets. Seek partner batch settlements, repeat usage, bundling, higher average baskets or fee renegotiation, without misleading customers.

Example C: 1,000 event bookings × AED 80 × 12% = AED 9,600 gross platform event fee/month. 400 luggage reservations × AED 25 × 20% = AED 2,000 commission. 100 Plus members × AED 29 = AED 2,900; 10 Pro organisers × AED 149 = AED 1,490; 2 sponsorships × AED 2,500 = AED 5,000. **Total illustrative monthly platform revenue = AED 20,990**, before processing charges, payroll, insurance, support, marketing, tax, refunds and all other costs. This is not a forecast or evidence of demand.

## A business model that scales without hurting communities

- Keep discovery, friendships, joining approved public circles and baseline trust/safety accessible.
- Monetise premium utility, high-quality curated experience production and economic activity the platform actually enables.
- A member subscription should reduce friction, not deny free members basic safety or support.
- Organisers should see measurable member retention, repeat bookings and saved admin hours before they are asked to pay.
- Sponsorship must be clearly labelled; never sell member contact details or hidden ranking manipulation.
- Reward points start closed-loop, non-cash and non-transferable. No crypto launch until specialist regulation review and genuine value are proven.

## The most important financial metrics

**GMV:** total booking value. Not AUNO revenue.

**Net platform revenue:** disclosed commissions, service charges, SaaS revenue and sponsorship earned under completed transactions, minus refunds/allowances as appropriate.

**Contribution margin:** net revenue minus payment fees, transaction-level support, acquisition/referral incentives, partner fees, chargebacks, insurance allocation and other variable costs.

**CAC:** fully allocated acquisition and onboarding cost / new activated customers. Track **activated** members, not mere signups.

**LTV:** conservative gross contribution per member × expected repeat transaction frequency × retention horizon, not revenue and not speculative infinite lifetime.

**Supply quality:** host attendance reliability, booking fill, complaints per 1000 check-ins, repeat participation and refund rate.

**North star:** verified members attending >=2 real activities in a rolling 30-day window, and a separate meaningful visitor experience completion measure.

## Commercial rollout

**Phase 0 — free beta:** 5–10 vetted community hosts; recurring activities manually organised and professionally managed; no payments handled by the app. Validate activation, safety and repeat rate.

**Phase 1 — event revenue:** payments through eligible UAE PSP; host contracts; clear organiser vs marketplace role; refunds/disputes; reconciliation and tax determination. Validate unit economics by category.

**Phase 2 — organiser SaaS:** enhanced scheduling, members, segmentation, reminder templates, reports, host reliability metrics and team roles. Charge only once retention and ROI are evident.

**Phase 3 — visitor offers:** packaged experiences with licensed operators, hotel referrals and itinerary convenience. Partner demand should be proven before large affiliate buildout.

**Phase 4 — luggage:** approved professional storage operators first, hospitality/café hosts only if lawful and insured. Full secure handoff/claims systems.

**Phase 5 — enterprise:** residential projects, universities, employers, coworking, private community operating systems, paid organiser infrastructure.

## Go/no-go checkpoints

- Retention: at least a defensible repeat-attendance cohort target defined before launch (test e.g. 30% within 30 days), with confidence by acquisition channel.
- Operations: reliable supply of real vetted activities, verifiable capacity and fast safety incident triage.
- Economics: positive contribution after processing, refunds and direct support *before* scaling paid acquisition.
- Trust: protected data, proper moderation and host screening; minimal cancellation surprises.
- Compliance: permitted event categories, appropriate licensed partners and support for VAT/invoicing obligations.

## Pricing experiments to run

1. Test host-paid versus attendee-paid fees with transparent all-in checkout totals.
2. Compare fee level against booking conversion, event attendance, host churn and net margin.
3. Offer free Organiser Starter, then Pro/Team at meaningful usage thresholds.
4. Trial premium trip value through an opt-in paid concierge, not through artificially restricted city information.
5. Test sponsor-funded member benefits that improve experiences, with clear paid placement labels.

## Real-world costs that won't be free

Business registration, licensed activities and approvals, sports/tourism permits, venue agreements, appropriate insurance, identity vendor fees, payment-processing fees, WhatsApp/SMS sending, refunds, customer support, operator staffing, security testing, data backups, taxes and possibly legal review. Free hosting is not a substitute for any of these.
